# How the BSc programme is organized

## Duration and Structure

The Bachelor of Science in Computer Science and Engineering is organized as a 4-year program [1][2][5][6]. The instruction is performed mostly in English [1][2]. The total degree requires the acquisition of 240 ECTS credits, distributed at a pace of 60 credits per year [2][6]. One credit corresponds to approximately 36 academic hours of student effort [6].

## Course Load by Year

Students take a total of 45 courses over the four years [2]. The distribution of core and elective courses varies by stream.

For the Software Engineering, Data Science, and Security-Blockchain streams, the yearly course distribution is identical [2]. These students take 12 core courses and 0 electives in the first year, 12 core and 0 electives in the second year, 10 core and 1 elective in the third year, and 6 core and 4 electives in the fourth year [3]. The total for these streams is 40 core courses and 5 elective courses [3].

For the Robotics stream, the distribution differs slightly [2]. Students take 12 core and 0 electives in the first year, 12 core and 0 electives in the second year, 11 core and 0 electives in the third year, and 8 core and 2 electives in the fourth year [4]. The total for this stream is 43 core courses and 2 elective courses [4].

## Tracks and Streams

The first two years contain fundamental courses in mathematics, physics, and computer science and engineering [1][5]. The body of knowledge includes Math, Physics, Algorithms and Complexity, Programming Languages, Software Development Fundamentals, Architecture and organization, Operating Systems, Computational Science, information management, and Networking and Communications [5]. The primary objective is to provide a solid scientific and technical foundation, fostering skills in efficient program development, problem-solving using computer programs, and understanding computer system theory [11].

In the third and fourth years, students select one of four streams [5][12]:
*   **Software Engineering:** Combines system theory, computer science, and software engineering for careers in software engineering [12].
*   **Data Science:** Integrates intelligent systems, computer science, and data science to evaluate and interpret big data [12].
*   **Robotics:** Provides fundamental knowledge in mechanics, mechatronics, electrical engineering, control theory, and robotics [12].
*   **Security-Blockchain:** Focuses on information security, secure software design, and distributed ledger technologies such as cryptocurrencies and blockchain [12].

The body of knowledge in the final two years includes Graphics and Visualization, Information Assurance and Security, Intelligent Systems, Parallel and Distributed Computing, Software Engineering, Systems Fundamentals, Human-Computer Interaction, Finance, Mechanical Engineering, Electrical Engineering, Mathematical Physics, Robotics, and Control Engineering [5].

## Internships

Every year, students have a mandatory internship during the summer break [8]. Internships typically start once the spring semester exam session is completed [8]. There are three types of internships:
*   **Industrial:** Work in Innopolis University partner IT companies, primarily in Innopolis [8].
*   **Scientific:** Work in university labs on ongoing research and development tasks [8].
*   **Administrative:** Work in university departments on IT analysis, design, and implementation projects [8].

Internships can also combine these three types [8]. The credit distribution for internships is 14 credits in the first year, 10 in the second, 10 in the third, and 24 in the fourth, totaling 58 credits [5]. An internship of 8 credits requires 288 hours of effort, approximately 7 weeks [6].

## Theses and Exams

At the end of the second year, students must pass a multidisciplinary exam to evaluate their comprehensive understanding of the discipline and ability to engage in system-oriented thinking [10]. This exam ensures students can solve broadly-based IT problems before progressing to years three and four [10].

## Language Requirements

Graduates are expected to know English at least at the level of IELTS 7 [9]. Admission requires a minimum IELTS level of 5 [9]. The university requires students to achieve specific English levels by the end of each year:
*   End of first year: IELTS 5.5 [9].
*   End of second year: IELTS 6 [9].
*   End of third year: IELTS 6.5 [9].
*   End of fourth year: IELTS 7 [9].

It is the student's responsibility to achieve these levels using university resources [9].

## Course Organization

Courses are organized using various teaching mechanisms: Class Lecture (Lc), Class Tutorial (Tut), Group Laboratory (Lab), and Individual Labs (IL) [7].

During the first two years:
*   Math and science courses typically follow the structure 2Lc-2Lab-2IL [7].
*   Computer science and engineering courses follow the structure 2Lc-2Tut-2Lab [7].
*   Technical courses follow the structure 2Lc-2Lab-2IL [7].
*   Humanities courses follow the structure 2Lc-4IL [7].
