# Recognition of earlier study results

## Who can apply and what can be recognized
You can apply for credit transfer if you are a student enrolled in the University's main professional educational programs, or if you are a parent or legal representative of a minor student [1][2][5]. The University recognizes results of study (subjects, courses, disciplines, modules, practice, or additional educational programs) that you completed at other educational organizations or within the University itself [1]. This Regulation is the authoritative source for credit transfer and overrides any conflicting course page information [1].

## Documents you must submit
You must submit an application and documents confirming your previous learning results to the University's Attestation Commission [2][6]. For results from other organizations, you must provide either an education or qualification document (including those from foreign countries) [3], or a study document such as a certificate of study, academic transcript, or similar document issued by any organization (including foreign ones) [4]. If you are applying for results from within the University at the same education level, the Commission uses the records already entered in your electronic grade book [5]. The application forms are listed in Appendix 1 and Appendix 2 to the Regulation [9]. You may submit your application and documents to the Commission in the forms specified in the Regulation [10]. Documents from foreign organizations must be submitted with a Russian translation [16]. Education or qualification documents from foreign countries must include a Russian translation in legalized form with a certificate of recognition, unless Russian legislation or an international treaty exempts you from recognition [17].

## Deadlines for submission
You must submit your application and confirming documents to the Attestation Commission no later than 14 calendar days before the start of the semester or trimester [6]. If you are a first-year student in your first semester or trimester, you must submit your application no later than 14 calendar days after the start of your studies [7]. If you participated in an academic mobility program, you must submit your application no later than 3 working days after completing the mobility program [8].

## How the commission evaluates your results
The Attestation Commission evaluates your results by comparing the planned learning outcomes of the part of the University's educational program you are currently studying with the learning outcomes you previously mastered [11]. The University grants credit only upon Establishment of Correspondence between these outcomes [12]. To establish correspondence, the Commission may conduct an Assessment of your actual achievement of the planned learning outcomes [13]. This procedure involves comparing your confirming documents with the planned outcomes for the relevant program part, and the Commission may analyze information from the issuing organization's official website or request information directly from that organization [14]. If you are applying for results from a foreign educational organization, the Commission establishes correspondence while accounting for that organization's credit unit calculation system, and may compare credit units with the volume of contact and independent work [15]. Recognition based on foreign study or education documents is conducted through Assessment according to the Regulation's Section 3 [18].

## Gaps and caveats
The Regulation does not specify the exact submission formats for applications and documents, only stating they can be submitted "in the following forms:" [10]. The text of Appendix 1 and Appendix 2 is not provided in the evidence [9]. The Regulation does not state what happens if you miss the 14-day or 3-day deadlines [6][7][8]. The Regulation does not define the specific criteria or scoring thresholds for "Establishment of Correspondence" or "Assessment" [12][13]. The Regulation does not specify how many credits or ECTS equivalents you will receive after a successful match [11][15]. The Regulation does not state whether you can appeal a negative decision or request a re-evaluation [12][13]. The Regulation does not clarify if partial credit is possible or if the entire course must match [11]. The Regulation does not specify the language requirements for non-Russian translations beyond the legalization rule [17]. The Regulation does not state how long the Commission takes to process your application [6]. The Regulation does not specify what happens to your submitted documents after the decision is made [2].
