# Grading scales across courses

## Grade scales and point ranges
103 of 275 courses state a grading scale with letter grades and point ranges, while 172 courses do not [5]. The most common scale is A 90–100, B 75–89, C 60–74, D 0–59, used by 54 of the 103 courses (52%) [6]. Among the 56 BSc courses with a scale, 31 use the most common scale [15]. Among the 40 MSc courses with a scale, 17 use it [16]. Among the 7 other program courses with a scale, 6 use it [17]. The remaining 49 courses use 21 different scales, and no other scale is used by more than 6 courses [7]. The lowest points required for an A are 90 in 60 courses, 85 in 22 courses, 80 in 12 courses, 92 in 2 courses, and 1 point each for 136, 95, 96, 104, 84, and 86 [8]. The lowest points for a B are 75 in 67 courses, 70 in 13 courses, 80 in 6 courses, 60 in 6 courses, 65 in 6 courses, and 1 point each for 102, 66, 84, 72, and 71 [9]. The lowest points for a C are 60 in 70 courses, 50 in 12 courses, 40 in 6 courses, 65 in 3 courses, 55 in 3 courses, 56 in 2 courses, 70 in 2 courses, 51 in 2 courses, and 1 point each for 68 and 45 [10]. The lowest points for a D are 0 in 102 courses [11]. Two courses give ranges in raw points where the top of grade A is above 100 [12]. Several scales feature overlapping or incomplete ranges, including A 85–100, B 75–84, C 60–75, D 0–59 (4 courses); A 136–170, B 102–135, C 68–101, D 0–68 (1 course); A 92–100, B 80–91, C 65–79, D 0–59 (2 courses); A 85–100, B 80–84, C 51–79, D 0–59 (2 courses); A 85–100, B 75–85, C 60–74, D 0–59 (5 courses); B 75–84 (1 course); and A 85–100, B 70–84, C 50–69, D 0–50 (1 course) [13].

## University regulation on grading
The university regulation maps letter grades to a five-point scale as A to «Excellent», B to «Good», C to «Satisfactory», D to «Fail», P to «Passed», and F to «Fail» [1]. Results of interim assessments A, B, C, and P are positive and cannot be retaken for improvement, except as specified in clause 6.1 of the regulation [2]. Results of interim assessments D and F are unsatisfactory results [3]. Teaching staff may use an alternative grading system during the teaching process [4]. When an alternative system is used, the grading system, evaluation criteria, and conversion rules must be specified in discipline syllabuses or internship programs and communicated to students in the university's electronic educational environment [4].

## Gaps and caveats
The evidence leaves open how the 172 courses without stated scales handle grading [5]. The regulation is silent on point ranges for letter grades, leaving that detail to individual course syllabuses [1][4]. The regulation does not specify whether alternative grading systems must align with the university's five-point scale mapping [1][4]. The evidence does not clarify what clause 6.1 specifies regarding retaking assessments [2]. The evidence does not explain why 2 courses use raw points above 100 or how overlapping ranges are resolved in grading [12][13]. The evidence does not state how many students are in each course or how grades are weighted [5][17]. Course pages that list point ranges go beyond the regulation by providing the specific thresholds the regulation leaves open [1][4].
