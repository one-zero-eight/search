# Eligibility and Duration Reduction for Accelerated Study

## Who qualifies for accelerated study
You qualify for accelerated study on an individual plan if you are enrolled in a bachelor’s or master’s program and you have secondary vocational, higher, or additional education, or you have studied or were studied in such programs, or you have abilities and development level that allow you to complete the program in a shorter time than the standard duration set by the University and federal standards [1]. The University makes the decision to carry out accelerated study for you [1].

You qualify for accelerated study on an individual plan if you are enrolled in an aspirantura program and you hold a diploma from aspirantura or adjuncture, a Candidate of Sciences diploma, a Doctor of Sciences diploma, or you are currently studying in another aspirantura program, or you have abilities and development level that allow you to complete the program in a shorter time than the standard duration set by the University and the educational standard [2]. The University makes the decision to carry out accelerated study for you [2].

## How the degree program duration is reduced
The evidence does not state a fixed number of semesters, months, or percentage reduction for your degree [3][4]. The reduction in duration is achieved through specific mechanisms rather than a predetermined shortened timeline [3][4].

For bachelor’s and master’s programs, the duration is reduced by crediting results of individual disciplines or modules, and/or individual practices that you completed during secondary vocational, higher, or additional education [3]. The reduction can also be achieved by increasing the pace of your program completion [3].

For aspirantura programs, the duration is reduced by crediting, in the form of re-examination, fully or partially the results of individual disciplines or modules, and/or individual practices, and/or individual types of scientific research work [4]. The reduction can also be achieved by increasing the pace of your program completion [4].

Increasing the pace of your program completion requires a personal application from you [5]. The University Attestation Commission makes a decision on your application [5]. The University Director issues an order to implement the increased pace [5]. This procedure applies only to students who have the corresponding abilities and development level [5].

## Gaps and caveats
The evidence does not specify the exact length of the shortened program, the number of credits or semesters saved, or the minimum duration required [3][4]. The evidence does not list the specific criteria or thresholds the Attestation Commission uses to evaluate your abilities and development level [5]. The evidence does not state whether the University decision is final or subject to appeal [1][2]. The evidence does not provide deadlines for submitting your personal application or for the Commission to review it [5]. The evidence does not explain how the crediting of prior disciplines, modules, or practices is calculated or how it directly translates into time saved [3][4]. If any course page or university portal states specific reduction periods, application deadlines, or evaluation criteria, those details are not covered by the provided regulation and should be verified separately. The regulation is silent on how the University calculates the exact shortened duration for individual students [3][4]. The regulation does not define what constitutes "corresponding abilities and development level" [5]. The regulation does not state whether the increased pace applies to all semesters or only specific periods [5]. All gaps remain open as the evidence provides no further details [1][2][3][4][5].
