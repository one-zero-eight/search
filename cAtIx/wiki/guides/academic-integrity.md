# Academic integrity and disciplinary measures

## What counts as a violation
Academic integrity consists of the behavioral rules in point 9 of the University's Internal Rules of Conduct and the ethical principles required to maintain trust in learning and research outcomes [5]. A violation is any action or attempt to deceive while mastering a discipline, during current control, or during interim and final assessments [5]. Plagiarism is using someone else's work in any form without citing sources and presenting it as your own [5]. If you refuse to allow your work to be checked for originality when a violation is suspected, the violation is considered established [8]. This regulation applies to all students enrolled in higher education programs at the University [3].

## How a case is handled
A teacher may set an expected similarity threshold for assignments in the course program or assignment notification [6]. The University uses plagiarism-checking software such as iThenticate, Turnitin, Antiplagiat, or other programs chosen by the teacher [7]. The teacher must inform you of the suspected violation [9]. If the originality check confirms the violation or you admit to it, the teacher must report the case to the Student Support and Development Department (SSDD) at the official email `319@innopolis.ru` [10][11]. In cases of plagiarism, disciplinary measures apply to all students involved in the violation [12].

## How to appeal
You have 3 working days after receiving an email from the SSDD about a disciplinary measure to appeal the violation and the measure [16]. To appeal, you must reply to the official SSDD email with your justification and evidence of your innocence [16]. If you do not submit an appeal within this deadline, the teacher's decision becomes final and the disciplinary measures are applied [17]. If you submit an appeal, the SSDD will schedule a Commission meeting via email and invite you within 10 working days of receiving your appeal [18]. At the meeting, you may present evidence and arguments for your defense, and the Commission may ask follow-up questions [19]. The Commission is a collegial body convened when you disagree with the violation fact [13]. It consists of at least 3 members, including administrative staff, faculty, and potentially student council representatives, with a recommended composition of an SSDD employee, the Dean or deputy of the Faculty of Computer and Engineering Sciences, 1 faculty member, a program manager if available, and an official student self-government representative [14]. The Commission makes decisions by simple majority vote [15].

## Disciplinary measures
The consequences for violations are disciplinary measures applied to you [20]. If you are caught violating the rules for the first time, you receive an unsatisfactory result (0-E) for the completed work, such as an assignment or interim assessment [21]. If you are caught violating the rules a second time, you receive an unsatisfactory result (0-Р) for the discipline or module (or for multiple disciplines if two cases occur on different subjects), you are directed to retake the interim assessment, and you receive an official warning [22].

## Gaps and caveats
The regulation does not specify the exact similarity thresholds that trigger a violation, only that a teacher may set one [6]. The definition of plagiarism is cut off in the source text, so the full scope of what constitutes plagiarism is not provided [5]. The regulation does not detail the exact procedures for how the Commission conducts its meetings or how evidence is formally submitted beyond the email invitation and verbal presentation [18][19]. It does not specify what happens after a second violation or if there are consequences for a third violation [22]. The regulation does not list the specific content of point 9 of the Internal Rules of Conduct that defines academic integrity [5]. Course pages or other university documents may contain additional information, but this regulation is the primary local act governing disciplinary measures for academic integrity violations [1][2].
