# Appeals and arrangements for students with disabilities in the final attestation of programmes without state accreditation

## Where these rules come from

These rules are part of the Regulation on the procedure for conducting the final attestation (итоговая аттестация) on bachelor's and master's higher education programmes that have no state accreditation, approved by order of the Director of 27 April 2024 No. ОД/АД-02/2024/04/27 [1]. The Regulation expressly covers the procedure for submitting and considering appeals, the change and (or) annulment of attestation results and the specific features of the attestation for students with health limitations [2].

The Regulation is in the wiki in two copies: the first carries an unrelated prefix in its title («Сведения о наличии оборудованных объектов для проведения практических занятий») [1], the second, made from the text layer of the same PDF, has the clean title [3], and their clauses read the same [3].

## The appeal commission

An appeal commission is created to consider appeals against attestation results [4]; it acts for one calendar year [5], its composition is approved no later than 1 month before the attestation begins [6], and it consists of its chair and not fewer than 3 members formed from the University's teaching staff who are not members of the final examination commissions [7].

The chair of the appeal commission is the Director of the University (the person acting as Director, or a person authorised by the Director by an administrative act) [8]. The chair organises and controls the commission's activity and ensures uniform requirements towards students [9]. A meeting is competent if not less than two thirds of the persons included in the commission take part, and decisions are taken by a simple majority, the chair having a casting vote when votes are equal [10], [11].

## Filing and hearing an appeal

- A student has the right to appeal the results of attestation tests [12].
- A written appeal is submitted to the appeal commission about a violation, in the student's opinion, of the established procedure of a test and (or) about disagreement with the results of the final examination [13]. It is submitted by the student in person no later than the next working day after the results are announced [14].
- For the appeal the secretary of the final examination commission sends to the appeal commission the minute of the final examination commission, the conclusion of its chair on compliance with procedural matters and, for an appeal about the final examination, the student's written answers if any, or, for an appeal about the defence, the final qualification work, the supervisor's review and the review(s) [15].
- The appeal is considered at a meeting of the appeal commission no later than 2 working days after it is submitted; the chair of the final examination commission and the student who appealed are invited, and the meeting may be held in the student's absence if the student does not appear [16].
- The decision is communicated to the student within 3 working days of the meeting, and the fact that the student has been made acquainted with the decision is certified by the student's signature [17].

## Decisions on appeals

- On a complaint about the procedure, the commission either rejects the appeal, if the reported violations are not confirmed and (or) did not affect the result of the test, or grants it, if the violations are confirmed and affected the result [18].
- Where the appeal is granted, the result of the test is annulled, the minute on the appeal is passed to the final examination commission no later than the next working day so that it implements the decision, and the student is given the opportunity to pass the test within the dates set by the University [19].
- On disagreement with the results of the final examination the commission either rejects the appeal and keeps the result or grants it and sets a different result [20]. The decision is passed to the final examination commission no later than the next working day and is the basis for annulling the earlier result and setting a new one [21].
- The decision of the appeal commission is final and not subject to review [22].
- A repeat test of a student who appealed is held in the presence of the chair or one of the members of the appeal commission and not later than the date of completion of studies at the University [23]; no appeal against the repeat test is accepted [24].

## Students with disabilities and health limitations: general rules

- For students from among disabled persons (инвалиды) the attestation is conducted taking into account the specific features of their psychophysical development, their individual capacities and their state of health (individual features) [25].
- General requirements: holding the attestation for disabled students in one room together with students who are not disabled, if this does not create difficulties for them or for the other students; the presence of an assistant or assistants giving the necessary technical help (to take a workplace, move about, read and format the task, communicate with the chair and members of the final examination commission); use of the necessary technical means; and unhindered access to rooms, toilets and other premises — ramps, handrails, widened doorways, lifts, with the room on the ground floor where there are no lifts, and special chairs and other devices [26], [27], [28], [29].
- All local acts of the University on the attestation are communicated to students from among disabled persons and persons with health limitations in a form accessible to them [30].

## Longer tests

On a written application of a disabled student the duration of a test may be increased [31]:

- the written final examination — by not more than 90 minutes [32];
- preparation of the answer at the oral final examination — by not more than 20 minutes [33];
- the student's presentation at the defence of the final qualification work — by not more than 15 minutes [34].

## Arrangements by category

Depending on the individual features of students with health limitations the University ensures the following requirements during a test [35]:

- For blind students: tasks and other materials are prepared in relief-dot Braille or as an electronic document accessible with specialised software for the blind or read out by an assistant [36], [37]; written tasks are done on Braille paper or on a computer with specialised software for the blind, or dictated to an assistant [38]; where necessary they are given a set of writing accessories and Braille writing paper, and a computer with specialised software for the blind [39].
- For visually impaired students: tasks and other materials are prepared in enlarged print [40], [41]; individual even lighting of not less than 300 lux is provided [42]; where necessary a magnifying device is provided, and students may use their own [43].
- For deaf and hard-of-hearing students and students with severe speech impairments: sound-amplifying equipment for collective use is provided and, where necessary, individual equipment [44], [45]; at their wish the tests are held in written form [46].
- For students with musculoskeletal disorders (severe impairment of the motor functions of the upper limbs or absence of the upper limbs): written tasks are done on a computer with specialised software or dictated to an assistant [47], [48]; at their wish the tests are held in oral form [49].

## Application for special conditions

A disabled student submits a written application on the need to create special conditions for the attestation tests, stating individual features, no later than 3 months before the attestation begins; documents confirming the individual features are attached (if the University does not already have them); the application states whether an assistant is needed and whether the duration of each test must be increased [50].
