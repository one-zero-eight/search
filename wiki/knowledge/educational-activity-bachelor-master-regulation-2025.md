# Organisation of bachelor's and master's programmes — the Regulation of 22 September 2025

## The document and what it governs

The Regulation on the procedure of organising and carrying out educational activity in higher education programmes — bachelor's programmes and master's programmes — of ANO VO «Innopolis University» was approved by order of the University of 22 September 2025 and signed by the rector A.V. Gasnikov, Innopolis, 2025 [1]. In the wiki copy the number of the order reads as «ОД/УД‐01/2025/09/22» inside garbled fragments, because the page is a text-layer extraction, so the number should be checked against the PDF [1].

The Regulation determines the rules of organising and carrying out educational activity in bachelor's and master's programmes, including the features of that activity for students with health limitations [2], and it is binding for such activity at the University [3]. It was drawn up on the basis of Federal Law No. 273-ФЗ of 29 December 2012, the order of the Ministry of Science and Higher Education No. 245 of 6 April 2021 on the procedure of organising educational activity in bachelor's, specialist and master's programmes, the order of the Ministry of Science and Higher Education and the Ministry of Education No. 885/390 of 5 August 2020 on practical training of students, the order of the Ministry of Education and Science No. 636 of 29 June 2015 on the procedure of state final attestation, the federal state educational standards of higher education, professional standards, the University's Charter and other federal and local acts [4].

## The programmes: how they are built

- Bachelor's programmes are delivered in bachelor's fields of study and master's programmes in master's fields of study; the lists of fields are approved by the Ministry of Science and Higher Education [5].
- The University may deliver one bachelor's or master's programme per field, several programmes with different profiles per field, or one programme across several fields [6].
- Programmes are developed and approved by the University itself; programmes that have state accreditation are developed in accordance with the federal state educational standards [7].
- The content of higher education and the conditions of study for disabled persons are determined, among other things, by the individual rehabilitation programme of the disabled person (if there is one), and for students with health limitations on the basis of programmes adapted where necessary for them [8].
- A programme is a set of the main characteristics of education (volume, content, planned results) and of organisational-pedagogical conditions, presented as a curriculum, a calendar study schedule, syllabuses of disciplines (modules), other components, assessment and methodological materials, and also as a working programme of upbringing, a calendar plan of upbringing work and forms of attestation; by decision of the University other materials may be included [9].
- Programmes in information security and programmes containing state secrets are developed and delivered in compliance with the legislation on information and on state secrets respectively [10], [11].
- Bachelor's programmes are open to persons with secondary general education, master's programmes to persons with higher education of any level [12]. Forms of study are set by the educational standards [13].
- Education is conducted in the state language of the Russian Federation unless the University's local acts provide otherwise; higher education may be obtained in a foreign language in accordance with the programme and the procedure established by legislation and local acts [14].
- Students are offered facultative disciplines (not obligatory for study) and elective disciplines (chosen obligatorily), as well as the possibility of obtaining several qualifications at the same time in the procedure of a local act; the elective disciplines chosen by a student are obligatory for study [15].

## Volume of a programme, credit unit, duration

The labour intensity of a programme (or of its part) in credit units characterises its volume; the volume of a part is a whole number of credit units; the volume and the annual volume of programmes are set by the educational standards; facultative disciplines are not included in that volume; when studying under an individual curriculum, including accelerated study, the annual volume is calculated without the volume of those components, including practical trainings, whose learning results were credited to the student, and may not exceed the volume set by the standard [16].

The University itself sets the size of a credit unit in astronomical or academic hours (with an academic hour of 40 or 45 minutes) within 24 to 30 astronomical hours; the size is uniform within a curriculum [17]. The terms of obtaining higher education by forms of study, with a network form of the programme, in accelerated study, and for disabled persons and persons with health limitations are set by the educational standards; education is obtained within those terms regardless of the educational technologies used [18].

The time of a student's academic leave, maternity leave and childcare leave until the child reaches the age of three is not included in the term of obtaining higher education if the student does not continue studying during that time [19].

## Periods of study, academic year and vacations

The educational process is organised by periods of study — academic years (courses), by periods within courses (semesters and/or trimesters) and/or by periods of mastering modules; the duration of a course includes study time and vacation time and may not exceed 366 calendar days; the University decides itself how periods within courses and module periods are allocated; with semesters or trimesters, each course has two semesters or three trimesters, and a course of less than 300 calendar days may have one semester or one or two trimesters; the process may run simultaneously by periods within courses and by module periods [20].

The academic year in the full-time form begins on 1 September; the University may postpone its start for the full-time and part-time (очно-заочная) forms by not more than two months, and for the other forms and for programmes delivered exclusively with e-learning and distance technologies it sets the start itself [21].

The total duration of vacations in an academic year, unless the educational standard provides otherwise, is [22]:

- not less than 49 and not more than 70 calendar days when study during the academic year lasts more than 300 calendar days [22];
- not less than 21 and not more than 49 calendar days when it lasts from 100 to 300 calendar days [22];
- not more than 14 calendar days when the academic year lasts less than 100 calendar days [22].

Educational activity under a programme is not carried out on non-working holiday days [23].

## How classes are organised

The University ensures the delivery of disciplines (modules) including ongoing assessment, the holding of practical trainings, interim assessment and final (state final) attestation [24]. Educational activity may take the form of contact work of students with teaching staff and (or) persons engaged by the University, of students' independent work, and, by decision of the University, of other forms including during practical training [25]; contact work may be conducted with e-learning and distance technologies [26].

Contact work includes lecture-type classes and (or) seminar-type classes (seminars, practical classes, practicums, laboratory work, colloquia and similar), and (or) group consultations, and (or) individual work of students with teaching staff including individual consultations; by decision of the University it also includes other classes and other forms of interaction with teaching staff, including during practical training, interim assessment and final attestation [27].

Classes are held according to the schedule; in drawing up schedules unreasonable expenditure of students' time is excluded; classes last not more than 90 minutes with breaks of not less than 5 minutes; the duration of classes in the form of practical training is set by the University itself [28].

For lecture-type classes study groups may be combined into streams, and by decision of the University groups of different fields may be combined into one stream; for seminar-type classes groups are formed from students of one field and the class is held for one group, and by decision of the University students of different fields may be combined into one group; the number of students in groups is set by the University itself, and for seminar-type classes groups are as a rule formed with not more than 30 students [29]. Groups for practical classes in physical culture and sport are formed with account of the state of health, physical development and physical fitness, as a rule with not more than 20 students [30].

## Transfer to another programme

On completion of the second course of a bachelor's programme, and by decision of the University at other times during the programme, the University may give students the opportunity to transfer to another programme delivered by the University on a competitive basis in the procedure set by the University's local acts [31].

## Approval and changes

The Regulation is approved by order of the director of the University or of a person authorised by the director [32], and changes or additions to it are introduced by the same order [33].
