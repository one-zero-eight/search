# Employee rights and the employer's rights and duties, including suspension from work

## The employer's rights

The employer has the right to conclude, change and terminate employment contracts in the procedure and on the conditions established by the Labour Code and other federal laws; to conduct collective negotiations and conclude collective agreements; to reward employees for conscientious and effective work; to require employees to perform their labour duties, to treat the employer's property (including third parties' property for whose safety the employer is responsible) and other employees' property carefully and to observe the Rules; to require observance of labour protection and fire safety rules; to hold employees disciplinary and materially liable in the procedure established by law; to adopt local normative acts; to create employers' associations; to exercise the rights provided by legislation on the special assessment of working conditions; to create a production council for preparing proposals on improving production activity and processes, introducing new equipment and technologies and raising labour productivity and employees' qualifications; and to require observance of the procedure of interaction with media representatives and of the use of the employer's name, trademark and symbols [1]. The employer may also exercise other rights granted by labour legislation [1].

## The employer's duties

The employer must observe labour legislation and other normative legal acts containing labour law norms, local normative acts, the conditions of the collective agreement (if any), agreements and employment contracts, and provide employees with the work stipulated by the contract [2].

On personal data, the employer must process the employee's personal data exclusively for the purposes of observing laws and other normative legal acts, helping employees with employment, education and career advancement, ensuring employees' personal safety, controlling the quantity and quality of work performed and ensuring the safety of property; obtain all personal data from the employee personally, and where data can only be obtained from a third party, notify the employee in advance, obtain written consent and inform about the purposes, supposed sources and methods of obtaining, the nature of the data and the consequences of refusing consent; not communicate personal data to a third party without the employee's written consent, except where necessary to prevent a threat to life and health or in other cases provided by the Code or federal laws; not communicate personal data for commercial purposes without written consent; warn recipients that the data may be used only for the purposes for which they were communicated and require confirmation of observance of confidentiality; transfer personal data within the organisation in accordance with a local act with which the employee is acquainted against signature [2].

The employer must also ensure safe working conditions meeting state labour protection requirements, provide employees with equipment, tools, technical documentation and other means needed to perform their duties, ensure equal pay for work of equal value, record the time actually worked by each employee, pay the wages due in full within the periods set by the Labour Code and the contracts, conduct collective negotiations and conclude a collective agreement, provide employees' representatives with full and reliable information for that purpose, acquaint employees with adopted local acts directly connected with their work, fulfil in time the orders of state supervision bodies and pay fines imposed, consider representations of trade union bodies and other elected representatives about violations and take measures to remove them, create conditions for employees' participation in managing the organisation, ensure everyday needs connected with the performance of duties, carry out mandatory social insurance, compensate harm caused in the performance of duties and moral harm, and suspend employees from work in the cases provided by law [2].

## Suspension from work

The employer must suspend an employee from work (not admit the employee to work) where the employee [3]:

- appears at work in a state of alcohol, narcotic or other toxic intoxication [3]
- has not passed the established training and knowledge testing in labour protection [3]
- has not passed the established mandatory medical examination or, where federal laws provide, the mandatory psychiatric assessment [3]
- has contraindications for performing the work stipulated by the contract, confirmed by a medical conclusion issued in the established procedure [3]
- has a special right (licence, right to drive a vehicle, right to carry arms, another special right) suspended for up to two months under federal laws, where this makes performance of the contract's duties impossible and the employee cannot be transferred with written consent to another available job (a vacant position or work corresponding to the employee's qualification, or a vacant lower or lower-paid position) that the employee can perform given their health [3]
- is subject to a demand of bodies or officials authorised by federal laws and other normative legal acts [3]
- falls under other cases provided by federal laws and other normative legal acts [3]

An employee who is a foreign citizen or a stateless person must also be suspended where the permit for attracting and using foreign workers is suspended or expires (for temporarily staying persons), where the work permit or patent expires (for temporarily staying persons), where the temporary residence permit or the temporary residence permit for education expires (for temporarily residing persons), where the residence permit expires (for permanently residing persons), and where the voluntary medical insurance contract or the employer's contract with a medical organisation on paid medical services for a highly qualified specialist expires, unless federal laws or international treaties provide otherwise [3].

An employee must also be suspended for the whole period of criminal proceedings until their termination or the entry into force of a court verdict, once law enforcement bodies report that the employee is prosecuted for the crimes named in the Labour Code [3]. The employer suspends the employee for the whole time until the circumstances that caused the suspension are removed, unless federal laws establish other rules of suspension [3].

## The employee's rights

The employee has the right to conclude, change and terminate the employment contract in the procedure and on the conditions established by the Labour Code and other federal laws; to be provided with the work stipulated by the contract; to a workplace meeting state labour protection requirements and the conditions of the collective agreement (if any); to timely and full payment of wages in accordance with qualification, complexity, quantity and quality of work; to rest ensured by the normal length of working time, reduced working time for certain professions and categories, weekly days off, non-working holidays and paid annual leaves; to full and reliable information about working conditions and labour protection requirements at the workplace, including the rights under legislation on the special assessment of working conditions; to training and additional professional education in the procedure established by the Labour Code and other federal laws; and to full information about their personal data and their processing [4].

The employee also has the right to free access to their personal data, including copies of any record containing them, except where federal law provides otherwise; to designate representatives for the protection of personal data; to require the exclusion or correction of incorrect or incomplete personal data and of data processed in violation of the Code or another federal law, and where the employer refuses, to state in writing their disagreement with justification, and to supplement evaluative personal data with a statement of their own point of view; to require the employer to notify all persons who previously received incorrect or incomplete data of the exclusions, corrections or additions; to associate, including to create and join trade unions; to participate in managing the organisation; to conduct collective negotiations and conclude collective agreements and agreements through representatives and to receive information on their fulfilment; to protect their labour rights, freedoms and lawful interests by all means not prohibited by law; to resolve individual and collective labour disputes, including the right to strike, in the procedure established by the Labour Code and other federal laws; to compensation of harm caused in the performance of duties and of moral harm; to mandatory social insurance in the cases provided by federal laws; and to exercise other rights provided by labour legislation [4].
