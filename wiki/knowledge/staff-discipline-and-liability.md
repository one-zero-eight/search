# Staff rewards, disciplinary and material liability (Правила внутреннего трудового распорядка)

## Rewards

To reward employees who conscientiously perform their labour duties, for long and impeccable work at the University and other successes in work, the employer may apply the following types of reward: announcing gratitude; issuing a bonus (incentive payments); awarding a valuable gift; awarding a certificate of honour; nominating for the title of best in the profession; nominating for state awards; and other forms provided by the University's local acts and the collective agreement (if any) [1]. The amount of the bonus is set within the limits provided by the local normative act on bonuses approved by the employer [2].

Other measures of reward are announced by order of the director of the University and brought to the knowledge of the whole staff; several types of reward may be applied at the same time [3].

## Disciplinary liability

For committing a disciplinary offence — non-performance or improper performance by the employee, through their fault, of the labour duties imposed on them — the employer may hold the employee disciplinary liable [4]. An employee may also be held disciplinary liable for violating the provisions of the employer's university culture set out in the Ethical Code of ANO VO «Innopolis University» [4].

The employer may apply the following disciplinary penalties: a remark (замечание); a reprimand (выговор); dismissal on the corresponding grounds provided by the Labour Code, the Federal Law «Об образовании в Российской Федерации» and other normative legal acts containing labour law norms [5].

- Only one disciplinary penalty may be applied for each disciplinary offence, and when imposing it the gravity of the offence and the circumstances in which it was committed must be taken into account [6].
- Before applying a penalty the employer must request a written explanation from the employee; if the explanation is not provided after two working days, a corresponding act is drawn up, and the absence of an explanation does not prevent the penalty [7].
- A disciplinary penalty may be applied to teaching staff for violating professional ethics, the Charter and/or other local acts of the employer on the basis of a written application (complaint), a copy of which must be given to the teacher; the consideration of the disciplinary offence and its results may be made public at the employer's discretion, taking into account the need to protect the interests of students [7].
- A penalty is applied not later than one month from the day the offence is discovered, not counting the time of the employee's illness, leave and the time needed to take account of the opinion of the employees' representative body [8]. A penalty may not be applied later than six months from the day the offence was committed, and on the results of an audit, inspection of financial-economic activity or audit, later than two years from its commission; the time of criminal proceedings is not included in these periods [8].
- The employer's order on the penalty is announced to the employee within three working days of its issue, not counting the time of the employee's absence from work; if the employee refuses to familiarise themselves with the order, a corresponding act is drawn up [9].
- The penalty may be appealed by the employee to the state labour inspectorate and/or the bodies considering individual labour disputes [10].
- If within a year from the day the penalty was applied the employee is not subjected to a new disciplinary penalty, the employee is considered not to have a penalty [11].
- Before the year expires the employer may lift the penalty on its own initiative, on the employee's request, on the petition of the direct head or of the employees' representative body (if any) [12].
- During the period of the penalty the employer may refrain from applying to the employee the measures of reward listed in point 11.1 [13].

## Material liability of the employee

The employer may hold the employee materially liable in the procedure established by the Labour Code and other federal laws [14]. The employment contract or written agreements attached to it may specify the material liability of the parties [15]. Termination of the contract after damage is caused does not release the employee from material liability [16].

The employee's material liability arises where the employee caused damage to the employer as a result of culpable unlawful behaviour (actions or inaction), unless the Labour Code or other federal laws provide otherwise [17]. An employee who caused direct actual damage must compensate it; unreceived income (lost profit) is not recovered from the employee [18].

The employee is released from material liability where the damage arose from force majeure, normal economic risk, extreme necessity or necessary defence, or from the employer's failure to perform the duty to ensure proper conditions for storing the property entrusted to the employee [19]. For the damage caused the employee bears material liability within their average monthly earnings, unless the Labour Code or other federal laws provide otherwise [20]. In the cases provided by the Labour Code or other federal laws the employee may be charged with full material liability, which consists in the duty to compensate the direct actual damage caused to the employer in full [21]. Written contracts on full individual or collective (brigade) material liability may be concluded with employees who have reached 18 and directly serve or use monetary, commodity valuables or other property [22].

The amount of damage caused by loss or damage of property is determined by actual losses calculated at the market prices in force in the locality on the day the damage was caused, but may not be lower than the value of the property according to accounting data with account of its wear [23]. Requesting a written explanation from the employee to establish the cause of the damage is mandatory, and if the employee refuses or evades giving it, a corresponding act is drawn up [24].

Recovery from the guilty employee of a sum not exceeding the average monthly earnings is made by the employer's order, which may be issued not later than one month from the final determination of the amount of damage [25]. If the month has expired, or the employee does not agree to compensate the damage voluntarily, and the sum to be recovered exceeds the average monthly earnings, recovery may be made only by a court [26].

The employee guilty of causing damage may compensate it voluntarily in full or in part; by agreement of the parties compensation by instalments is allowed, the employee giving the employer a written obligation stating the payment dates, and if the employee who gave such an obligation is dismissed and refuses to compensate, the unpaid debt is recovered in court [27]. With the employer's consent the employee may transfer equivalent property or repair the damaged property [28]. Compensation of damage is made regardless of holding the employee administratively, disciplinarily or criminally liable for the actions or inaction that caused it [29].

If the employee is dismissed without valid reasons before the end of the period set by the employment contract or the agreement on training at the employer's expense, the employee must reimburse the training costs incurred by the employer, calculated in proportion to the time not worked after the training, unless the contract or the training (apprenticeship) agreement provides otherwise [30].

## Material liability of the employer

The employer's material liability arises where damage is caused to the employee as a result of culpable unlawful behaviour (actions or inaction), unless the Labour Code or other federal laws provide otherwise [31]. The employer compensates the damage in accordance with the Labour Code and other federal laws [32], and the contract or written agreements attached to it may specify the employer's material liability [33].

The employer must compensate the employee for earnings not received in all cases of unlawful deprivation of the opportunity to work, for damage caused to the employee's property, and is liable for delay in paying wages and other amounts due [34]. Damage to the employee's property is compensated in full, the amount being calculated at the market prices in force on the day of compensation, and with the employee's consent the damage may be compensated in kind [35].

The employee sends the employer an application for compensation of damage; the employer must consider it and take a decision, and if the employee disagrees with the decision or receives no answer in the established period, the employee may go to court [36]. Where the employer violates the established period of payment of wages, payment for leave, payments on dismissal and/or other amounts due, it must pay them with interest (money compensation) of not less than 1/150 of the key rate of the Bank of Russia in force at that time on the amounts not paid in time for each day of delay, starting from the day following the established payment date and ending with the day of actual settlement; where the payment was made only in part, the interest is calculated on the amounts actually not paid in time [37]. Moral harm caused to the employee by unlawful actions or inaction of the employer is compensated in money in the amounts determined by agreement of the parties to the employment contract [38].
