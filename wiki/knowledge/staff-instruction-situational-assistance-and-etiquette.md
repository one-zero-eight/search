# Working with disabled people — ethics of communication and situational assistance (Instruction of 16 December 2025)

## Principles

The University's Instruction for employees on working with disabled people treats observance of ethical rules of communication as an important part of ensuring accessibility of buildings, structures and services [1]. The main principle of giving help is not to do harm through unforeseen risks and circumstances, and the person is to be treated and respected as one would treat oneself [2].

Employees of organisations providing services to the population must help disabled people overcome the barriers preventing them from receiving services on an equal basis with others; the set of abilities, knowledge and skills needed for effective communication in giving that help is called communicative effectiveness [3]. Its skills are avoiding conflict situations, listening attentively and hearing the person, regulating one's own emotions during interaction, ensuring a high culture and ethics of relations, and resisting manipulation in a civilised way [3].

## General rules of etiquette

- When talking with a disabled person, address the person directly, not the accompanying person or the sign-language interpreter present at the conversation [4].
- It is natural to shake hands when introduced, including a hand that is hard to move or a prosthesis — right or left [4].
- When meeting a person who sees poorly or not at all, name yourself and the people who came with you; in a group conversation say whom you are addressing and name yourself [4].
- If you offer help, wait until it is accepted, and then ask what and how to do [4].
- Treat adults as adults; address them by name and use «ты» only if you know them well [4].
- Do not lean on or hang on a wheelchair: it is part of the person's untouchable space [4].
- When talking with a person who has difficulties in communication, listen attentively, be patient and wait until the person finishes the phrase; do not correct or finish it for them, and never pretend you understand when you do not — repeat what you understood [4].
- When talking with a person using a wheelchair or crutches, place yourself so that your eyes are on one level with theirs; with a person who can read lips, place yourself so that the light falls on you and you are clearly visible, with nothing (food, cigarettes, hands) in the way [4].
- To attract the attention of a person who hears poorly, wave your hand or pat the shoulder, look straight into the eyes and speak clearly, bearing in mind that not all people who hear poorly can read lips [4].
- Do not be embarrassed if you accidentally say «See you» or «Have you heard about…?» to a person who cannot see or hear [4].

## Rules for particular groups

For people with difficulties in movement the Instruction requires, among other things [5]: not to lean on, push or put feet on the wheelchair without permission; always to ask whether help is needed before giving it, for instance to open a heavy door; if help is accepted, to ask what to do and follow the instructions; to wheel the chair slowly at first; to check personally in advance that the places of planned events are accessible and which barriers may arise; not to pat the person on the back or shoulder; to sit at the same level; not to stand behind or beside the person; to watch the surroundings unobtrusively, hold the door, let the person pass through a narrow door first and not walk faster than the companion; to warn about architectural barriers in advance; and to remember that people with difficulties in movement usually have no problems with sight, hearing or understanding, and that using a wheelchair is a way of moving freely, not a tragedy [5].

For blind people and people with visual impairments [6]: when offering help, guide the person without squeezing the hand and without grabbing and pulling them; briefly describe where you are and warn about obstacles — steps, puddles, pits, low lintels, pipes; use phrases about sound, smell and distance and share what you see; do not command, touch or play with a guide dog; warn before reading to the person, speak in a normal voice and do not omit information unless asked; read a document aloud when the person must sign it, since disability does not free a blind person from the responsibility arising from the document; always address the person directly, even if they do not see you, not the sighted companion; name yourself and introduce the other people present and say if you want to shake hands; when offering a seat, direct the hand to the back or armrest rather than seating the person, and let them touch the object freely; name whom you address in a group of blind people; warn when you move away; the word «смотреть» is normal, meaning to perceive by touch; avoid vague definitions and give precise instructions such as «the glass is in the middle of the table»; if the person has lost the route, come and help instead of directing from a distance; lead the person perpendicular to the steps, without jerks, and do not put your hands behind your back while guiding [6].

For people with hearing impairments [7]: look straight at the person, do not shade or cover your face with hands, hair or objects; ask which way of communicating the person prefers; speak louder and more clearly or lower the pitch of the voice as needed; attract attention by name, a slight touch or a wave; speak clearly and evenly, without excessive emphasis and without shouting, especially into the ear; when asked to repeat, rephrase the sentence and use gestures; make sure you are understood; write down numbers, technical terms and addresses or send them by other means; ask whether it would be easier to correspond; remember that large or crowded rooms, bright sun and shadow are barriers; when communicating through an interpreter, address the conversation partner, not the interpreter; ask at the first meeting whether the person reads lips, and if so look at the face, speak clearly and slowly, use simple phrases and avoid inessential words, using facial expression, gestures and body movements to clarify the meaning [7].

Separate rules are also given for people with developmental delay, communication problems and mental disorders [8], [9] and for people with difficulties in speech [10].

## Situational assistance: what it is

Situational assistance is help given to a disabled person in order to overcome the barriers preventing them from receiving all services provided to the population on an equal basis with others [11]. The necessary help is given on entering the building and leaving it and when handing over and putting on outer clothing; first of all it is clarified what help is needed, the purpose of the visit to the organisation and whether accompaniment is needed [12].

## The general algorithm

To ensure access of disabled people to services and to the objects where they are provided, the Instruction requires [13]:

- telling the person about the architectural features of the building — the number of floors, the main structural subdivisions and where they are, in which office to address which question, and where the hygiene rooms and toilets are [13]
- telling the person, according to the degree of their social restrictions, about the accessible environment — ramps, mnemonic schemes, tactile paths, handrails and tactile marks — with special attention to danger signs such as sound alarms and light boards, and teaching emergency evacuation and the fastest and safest ways out of the building [13]
- conducting a tour of the object, drawing attention to sanitary rooms, stairs, ramps, the canteen or buffet and rest places (hall, foyer, green corner), and where necessary showing how doors and sanitary fittings open and close and where the emergency call buttons are [13]
- introducing the person personally to all the University's specialists involved in working with them, presenting the specialist and the person to each other by full name, and informing whom to address in all cases of difficulty [13]

## Assistance by group

For a person with difficulties in movement, situational assistance in the University's study and production premises covers entry and exit, movement inside the building and up and down stairs, the cloakroom (help to undress and dress, including inside the room), the canteen and buffet (to take out the necessary dish and put it on the table) and, where necessary, visiting the toilet [14]. Employees of the department of placement organisation and the reception administrators go out into the street when a person with restricted movement appears, open the entrance doors, provide a wheelchair to a person on crutches where necessary and help to transfer into it, help on entering, inform the assistant (помощник) about the visit, and where necessary help to move inside, accompany to the offices and to the cloakroom, help to dress and undress and help on leaving; the assistant helps with the necessary documents including appeals to the University's administration on questions of mastering the programmes, with orientation in the University's infrastructure, with everyday questions of students living in the hostels and with questions of organising places of practice for students [15].

For a person with a visual impairment the same range of assistance is given [16]. The staff meet the blind visitor in the street or at the entrance, open the entrance doors and help to enter, take the visitor by the elbow and accompany to the assistant, introducing the inscriptions in the University where necessary, help to dress and undress, accompany to the needed office holding the elbow, help on leaving, help with walking a guide dog and with orientation in the room; the assistant helps to become acquainted with the University's local acts, with the documents, orientation, everyday questions of students in the hostels and the organisation of places of practice [17].

For a person with full or partial absence of hands, assistance is given in all actions performed with the hands: entry and exit, opening and closing doors; the cloakroom and the sports hall changing room (help to change into sports clothes); the hostel rooms (help to comb and shave) and all actions performed with the hands; and paying for services on the University's territory and completing the necessary documentation [18]. The staff meet the person, open the doors, help to enter, invite the assistant, help to dress and undress, accompany to the office holding the elbow and help on leaving [19].

A person with a hearing impairment needs the services of a sign-language interpreter for formal communication; without an interpreter, situational assistance is given in all rooms where there is no written information about the University's services [20]. During the person's stay the interpreter accompanies them on the University's territory, introduces the written information about the events held there and takes part in rehabilitation measures by providing sign-language interpretation [21]; without an interpreter the assistant (помощник) introduces the written information about the procedure for providing educational services and other information the person requests [22].
