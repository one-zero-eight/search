# Academic integrity and disciplinary measures

## What counts as a violation

Academic integrity consists of the behavioral rules described in point 9 of the University's Internal Rules of Conduct and the ethical principles that participants in the educational process must follow to maintain trust in learning outcomes and research achievements [1].

A violation is any action or attempt at deception while mastering a discipline, during current control, or during interim and final assessments [1].

Plagiarism is using someone else's work in any form without citing sources and presenting it as your own [1]. Plagiarism includes:

- Using exact words of another student [1].
- Copying materials from the Internet or other electronic and print resources without proper citation formatting, quotation marks, and footnotes [1].
- Accepting more help from another person than necessary when writing a work [1].

The similarity volume is a number determined by software that must not exceed a set threshold. The threshold value is set by the teacher for each discipline and communicated to students. Similarity may include quotations, in-text citations, names, titles, terms, and code [1]. Similarity may be measured pairwise between students' works or against other datasets [1].

If a student refuses to allow their work to be checked for originality when a violation is suspected, the violation is considered established [2].

This regulation applies to all students enrolled in higher education programs at the University [3]. A teacher (преподаватель) is the person responsible for teaching a discipline (module) or a practice [1].

## What point 9 of the Internal Rules requires

The tasks students receive in the study process must be performed individually, without joint work or its division, unless something else is specially agreed when the task is issued [4]. If the University finds that students have passed their results of work to other students, both those passing and those receiving are recognised as having breached the Rules as regards the integrity policy, and the results of their work are annulled [5]. While doing tasks it is forbidden to involve third persons or to use outside resources, except the resources named in the working programmes of the subjects, courses, disciplines (modules), the programmes of practice and the programmes of final (state final) attestation; tasks are done by students independently and in full unless something else is specially agreed when the task is issued [6].

Team work on tasks is carried out exclusively in the directions and within the limits set in advance by the teacher, and a collective task must contain a direct indication of joint work and the list of participants [7]. External sources, including printed and electronic materials, among them those obtained from the Internet, may be used in doing a task only with the teacher's direct permission; if such sources were used, the result must contain an explicit reference to the sources of the ideas, algorithms, texts, codes, experimental results and so on [8]. Work and (or) tasks done by a student in one discipline (module) cannot be used in doing tasks in other disciplines (modules) [9].

During interim and final (state final) attestation, tasks must be performed independently without using any information technologies or materials, unless the requirements for holding the attestation establish otherwise; during the attestation it is forbidden to exchange tasks, to pass on the results of solutions, or to acquaint other students with the tasks and the way to solve them, and the results of those who breach these requirements are annulled [10]. So that the attitude to the study process is conscientious and knowledge is assessed objectively, students must not take any actions aimed at obtaining information about the content of attestation tests and other measures aimed at assessing knowledge before those are held [11].

Correspondingly, a student is obliged not to allow violations of intellectual (copyright and related, inventive, patent) rights during study, including appropriation of authorship (plagiarism), as well as copying (списывание), double submission, fraud, copying and fabrication of data and results of work [12].

## Checking originality

A teacher may set an expected similarity threshold for assignments in the course program or assignment notification [13].

The University may use the following plagiarism-checking programs: iThenticate, Turnitin, Antiplagiat, or other programs at the teacher's discretion [14].

## Procedure when a violation is detected

The teacher must inform the student of the suspected violation [15].

If the originality check confirms the violation, or the student admits the violation themselves, the disciplinary measures described in section 7 "Disciplinary measures" must be applied to the student [16].

If the originality check confirms the violation or the student admits to it, the teacher must report the case to the Student Support and Development Department (SSDD) at the official email 319@innopolis.ru [17].

In cases of plagiarism, disciplinary measures apply to all students involved in the violation [18].

The SSDD informs students of disciplinary measures by sending a letter to their corporate email [19].

## Appeals

A student may appeal the violation and the disciplinary measure within 3 working days after receiving the SSDD email [20]. To appeal, the student must reply to the official SSDD email with justification and evidence of innocence [20].

If no appeal is submitted within this deadline, the teacher's decision becomes final and disciplinary measures are applied [21].

Within 10 working days of receiving the appeal, the SSDD schedules a Commission meeting via email and invites the student [22].

The student may present evidence and arguments in their defense, and the Commission may ask follow-up questions [23].

The Commission decides whether to accept or reject the appeal. The decision is final [24].

Any falsification of data or false statements by the student are treated as a separate violation, and the Commission may apply additional disciplinary measures [25].

The SSDD communicates the Commission meeting results to the student during the meeting and via email [26].

## The Commission

The Commission on Dispute Resolution for Academic Integrity is a collegial body that resolves disputes regarding academic integrity violations [27]. It is convened when a student disagrees with the fact of a violation [27].

The Commission consists of at least 3 people, including representatives of administrative staff, teaching faculty, and possibly representatives of the Student Council [28]. A recommended composition includes an SSDD employee, the Dean of the Faculty of Computer Science and Engineering (or a deputy), one teaching faculty member, a program manager (if available), and an official student self-government representative [28].

Decisions are made by simple majority vote [29].

## Disciplinary measures

If a student is caught violating the rules for the first time, they receive an unsatisfactory result (0–E) for the completed work (assignment, interim assessment, etc.) [30].

If a student is caught violating the rules a second time, they receive an unsatisfactory result (О–Р) for the discipline or module (or for multiple disciplines if two cases are discovered on different subjects), are directed to retake the interim assessment, and receive an official warning [31].

If a student commits a serious violation a third time (at any point during their studies, regardless of course), they are expelled from the University for failing to fulfill obligations to responsibly master the educational program and for failing to complete the curriculum [32].

The teacher and the SSDD may recommend that the student perform corrective actions, such as visiting a psychologist [33].

The SSDD records and maintains a register of students with academic integrity violations [34].

## The document behind these rules

The regulation on the application of disciplinary measures for violations of academic integrity was approved by order No. ОД/АД-03/2023/02/01 of 1 February 2023 and put into effect on 1 February 2023 [35]. The order covers the University's higher education programmes: bachelor's programmes, master's programmes, higher-qualification programmes, and programmes preparing scientific and scientific-pedagogical staff in aspirantura [35].

To inform students about its charter documents, local normative acts and study documentation the University publishes those documents on its official website and lets students read them, and the changes and amendments to them, through student information channels such as Telegram mailings or the Outlook corporate mail [36].
