# The curriculum and the calendar study schedule of a bachelor's or master's programme

## Where these rules come from

The calendar study schedule and the curriculum are parts of a bachelor's or master's programme under the Regulation on the procedure of development and approval of bachelor's and master's programmes, approved by order of 2 August 2022 No. ОД/АД-02/2022/08/02 (in force from 1 September 2022) [1], [2].

## The calendar study schedule

The calendar study schedule is part of the programme [3]. It is developed in accordance with the requirements of the federal state educational standard and shows the sequence of implementation of the programme by years of study, including theoretical study, internships, interim attestations and the state final attestation, and holidays [4].

The duration of a course includes the time of study and the time of holidays and cannot exceed 366 calendar days; the University determines by itself the periods of study within courses and the periods of mastering modules [5]. The schedule indicates the periods of the types of study activity, of interim attestations, of the final (state final) attestation, of holidays and the non-working public holidays [6], and educational activity under the programme is not conducted on non-working public holidays [7].

The start of the academic year is established by an order of the University director or a person authorised by the director [8].

The total duration of holidays during the academic year, unless the federal state educational standard establishes otherwise, is [9]:

- with an academic year of more than 300 calendar days — not less than 49 and not more than 70 calendar days [9];
- with an academic year of not less than 100 and not more than 300 calendar days — not less than 21 and not more than 49 calendar days [9];
- with an academic year of less than 100 calendar days — not more than 14 calendar days [9].

The calendar study schedule for the current academic year is drawn up as a separate document and approved by an order of the University director or another responsible person authorised by the director before the academic year begins [10]; it is placed in the University's electronic information-educational environment [11]. Under study by an individual curriculum, including an accelerated one, the calendar study schedule is part of the individual curriculum [12]. Model calendar study schedules for the full-time and part-time forms of study are given in annex 1 to the model form of the programme [13].

## The curriculum

The curriculum is part of the programme [14]. It is the document that determines the list, labour intensity, sequence and distribution by study periods of disciplines (modules), internships and other types of study activity and, unless the federal law on education establishes otherwise, the forms of interim assessment of students [1].

The curriculum is developed in accordance with the federal state educational standard for the whole period of study and is in force, taking its actualisation into account, throughout the whole term of study of the students of the corresponding year of admission [15].

When forming the curriculum the University determines the list and labour intensity of disciplines (modules) and internships, their distribution by study periods, the correspondence between the competences formed by the programme and the planned learning outcomes in disciplines (modules) and internships, the types of internships, and the hours allocated to contact work, assessment and independent work of students in disciplines (modules) and internships [16].

A bachelor's programme must ensure the implementation of disciplines (modules) in philosophy, history (history of Russia, general history), a foreign language and life safety within Block 1 «Disciplines (modules)», and also the implementation of disciplines (modules) in physical culture and sport [17]:

- in the volume of not less than 2 credit units within Block 1 «Disciplines (modules)» [17];
- in the volume of not less than 328 academic hours which are obligatory for mastering, are not converted into credit units and are not included in the volume of the bachelor's programme, within elective disciplines (modules) in the full-time form of study [17].

The educational process under the programme is organised by academic years (courses) and by study periods allocated within courses — semesters or trimesters; an academic year consists of two semesters or three trimesters, each ending with the forms of interim assessment provided by the curriculum, and within a course of less than 300 calendar days one semester or one or two trimesters may be allocated [18].

The curriculum states the disciplines (modules), internships, attestation tests of the state final certification and other types of study activity with their volume in credit units, their sequence and distribution by study periods [1]. It distinguishes the volume of students' work in interaction with the teacher (contact work by types of study sessions, assessment) and of students' independent work in academic hours [1], and states the form of interim assessment for each discipline (module) and internship [1].

## Blocks, parts and elective disciplines

The structure of the programme includes the following blocks: Block 1 «Disciplines (modules)», Block 2 «Practice» and Block 3 «State final certification» [1]. The volume of each block is established in accordance with the federal state educational standard [19].

The programme consists of an obligatory part and a part formed by the participants of educational relations [1], and the share of the obligatory part, without the volume of the state final certification, in the total volume of the programme is determined in accordance with the federal state educational standard [1]. When the programme is developed, students are provided with the opportunity to master elective disciplines (modules) and optional disciplines (modules) [20].

## How the curriculum is built and approved

The development of the curriculum at the University may be carried out with the use of an appropriate automated system [1]. The curriculum is agreed by the dean of the faculty, by the vice-rector — head of the department of academic policy and organisation of educational activity and by the vice-rector for quality of education, and is approved by the University's Academic Council as part of the educational programme [1].

The curriculum includes the following sections [21]:

- a title page with the approval stamp, data on the level of preparation, the code and name of the field of study, the focus (profile) of the programme, the faculty and department, the form of study, the term of obtaining education, the educational standard on which the programme is based, the list of areas and/or spheres of professional activity and professional standards, and the list of types of professional tasks [22];
- a summary curriculum with the distribution of disciplines (modules), internships and attestation tests of the state final certification by blocks and parts of the programme, their indices and names, the form of interim assessment, the total labour intensity in credit units and academic hours, the total hours of contact and independent work, the labour intensity in credit units by each course and semester, and the code and name of the department attached to each discipline (module) [23];
- the curriculum itself, with the same data plus the hours of contact and independent work by each course and semester and the list of competences formed by each discipline (module), internship and attestation test [24];
- the list of disciplines (modules) for each competence established for the programme [25];
- the list of professional standards and generalised labour functions by competences [26];
- summary values: the total volume of the programme and its volume by courses and semesters with and without optional disciplines (modules), the distribution of the volume by blocks and parts, the study load, contact work during theoretical study, total contact work, class load, obligatory forms of assessment, the percentage of lectures in class work, the volume of the obligatory part in the total volume, and the volume of contact work in the total time for disciplines (modules) [27];
- data on the disciplines (modules) and internships of the first course with their labour intensity, the hours of contact and independent work and the study load [28], and the same for the second course [29], the third course where it exists [30], the fourth course where it exists [31] and the fifth course where it exists [32];
- data on internships [33].

Individual curricula are formed on the basis of the curriculum, which in such cases is considered the basic one in relation to the individual curricula being formed [34].
