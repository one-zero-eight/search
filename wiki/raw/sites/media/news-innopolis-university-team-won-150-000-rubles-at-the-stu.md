# Innopolis University team won 150,000 rubles at the student programming contest

> Страница относится к 2015 году: сведения на ней могли устареть.

9 декабря 2015

The GS Group contest was dedicated to the creation of a new Android game application.

![](https://media.innopolis.university/upload/iblock/7cb/dI0Sqc_4exU.jpg)

The finals of the GS Group National Student Programming Contest had finished in Saint-Petersburg the day before. Yury Gavrilin and Vladislav Kurenkov, first-year bachelor students of Innopolis University took part in the event. According to the results of the pitch-session, the jury had chosen three finalists. Each winner got RUB 150 000 as the prize money.

The first GS Group programming contest for the first-to-fourth year students of Russian universities was dedicated to the creation of a new Android OS game application. Participants had three months to develop a game prototype inspired by “Back to the Future”. Developers of ten applications, who earlier gained the best points, took part in the final pitch-session. The final presentation was held in Saint-Petersburg, an expert panel consisted of representatives of GS Group, ITMO University, Saint Petersburg State University, Program Systems Institute of the Russian Academy of Sciences, the “RUSSOFT” Association of Software Development Companies and “Sperasoft”, the largest game-developer in the Eastern Europe

According to the contest results, prize for the best project presentation went to the Innopolis University students. They introduced a project of a multilevel arcade called “Johnny is Late for the Dinner”.

Elvira Gizatullina, Deputy Director for Strategic Development at GS Group comments: “The first national programming contest is another project of a complex educational program by GS Group. Students were offered to try their hand in the project development for the video game industry, which is a popular area among modern IT-companies. It wasn’t enough to provide a high-quality code and suggest a unique gameplay to win the contest. Teams had to make a convincing presentation of their project to the audience . The contest was aimed to identify talented students having skills and knowledge that are in demand at theIT-market.”

In total, the contest was attended by 93 teams from every part of Russia: Innopolis, Saint-Petersburg, Moscow, Krasnoyarsk, Kazan, Kursk, Volgograd, Izhevsk and etc.

Yury Gavrilin, a first-year bachelor student of Innopolis University and one of the contest winners shared his impressions: “I`m grateful to Innopolis University for the opportunity to participate in this wonderful contest. I want to say that these active and motivated people, who study at our University made the development of this game real. I want to thank the University for providing all the necessary conditions to work on the project within the last three months.”
