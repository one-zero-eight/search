# Innopolis University students presented automated chemical laboratory on Science Hack Day#2

> Страница относится к 2015 году: сведения на ней могли устареть.

18 октября 2015

<p>The event took place in Saint Petersburg, Russia on October 16-18, 2015. </p>

![](https://media.innopolis.university/upload/iblock/0f8/shd.jpg)

A team of five students (two MSIT program students and three students of Bachelor’s program) presented Innopolis University on Science Hack Day #2, which took place in Saint Petersburg, Russia, on October 16-18, 2015.

[Science Hack Day](http://sciencehackday.org/) is the series of events taking place all over the world: in Japan, USA, Mexico, Brazil, Kenya, South Africa, Germany and other countries. The Russian [Science Hack Day](http://hackday.ru/sciencehackday-2/report) was held as 48-hour-all-night hackathon. This interdisciplinary event aims to create technology projects and popularize creativity in science.

Within 48 hours, Innopolis University team created and presented a prototype of an automated chemical laboratory. This project works toward experiments automation, which requires multiple repetitions and rather precise control. Robotic system created by Innopolis University team aims to optimize testing process and reactions monitoring. To realize their idea, robotics graduate students Andrey Getmanskiy and Dmitry Popov applied in practice their knowledge of the direct and inverse kinematics of manipulators, which they had obtained at "Introduction to robotics” course. This course is taught by Professor Evgeni Magid and TA Aidar Gabdullin, and it introduces students to the basics of robotics.

“Our team was really standing out from “the crowd” as no one could expected to hear the sounds of metal hacksaw, and more surprising was the fact that we managed to create a workable robotic arm during the hackathon. We created the manipulator, which was able to relocate flasks with chemical reagents along the pre-programmed trajectory and deliver them to the experimental test bench. For some manipulator components, we used 3D printing. Our team also designed reagents supply system, magnetic stirrer and mobile application for remote monitoring of the experiment process. All these were completed within just 48 hours!” – shared his impressions MSIT-robotics program student Andrey Getmanskiy.
