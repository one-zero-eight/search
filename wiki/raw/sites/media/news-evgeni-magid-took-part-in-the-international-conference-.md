# Evgeni Magid took part in the International Conference on Intelligent Robots and Systems IROS-2015

> Страница относится к 2015 году: сведения на ней могли устареть.

9 октября 2015

The event took place at the Congress Center Hamburg, September 28th – October 2nd, 2015.

![](https://media.innopolis.university/upload/iblock/c46/IMG_5964.JPG)

Francesco Ferro, CEO of PAL Robotics and Prof. Evgeni Magid at PAL Robotics company stand

The annual International Conference on Intelligent Robots and Systems (IROS) is one of the two major events for roboticists from around the world. The conference brings together leading scientists and researchers as well as representatives of the largest robotics companies. This year the conference program included lectures of invited professors, reports, stand presentations of scientific papers, posters, seminars, showcases of innovation products from robotics companies, and experimental developments of universities and research institutes.

The theme of the [IROS 2015](http://www.iros2015.org/) International Conference was “Gateway to Era of Robots” what is quite symbolic considering that Hamburg, the host city of the conference, is often referred to as the “Gateway to the World”.

At IROS-2015, Professor Evgeni Magid, Head of the Intelligent Robotic Systems Laboratory (LIRS) presented the research results on AR-601M robot locomotion modelling. His report “Modelling walking primitives approach in Simulink for Russian anthropomorphic robot AR-601M” became a part of [Perception and Planning for Legged Robot Locomotion in Challenging Domains](https://iros2015wsperceptionandplanning.wordpress.com/program/) Workshop on October 2, 2015.

“Workshop reports of colleagues from [Stanford](https://www.stanford.edu/) and [MIT](http://web.mit.edu/) with demonstrations of their bipedal walking robots models and physical simulators have shown that Russian developments in this area are far behind of those made by the leading international universities. This came with no surprise since both universities have been receiving quite generous research funding from public and private institutions for decades. Meanwhile, an efficient grants and subsidy system in Russia just have started to gain in its strength. So far, unfortunately, excessive reporting and justification letters writing requirements demonstrate the mistrust of grantors to scientists (which receive funding) and consume significant time. Despite these challenges, Russian developments have excited the foreign colleagues. I believe that to a certain extent, we are in better situation than Stanford or MIT: our laboratory is young (we just have celebrated our first anniversary on October 2nd!) and ambitious, we are not constrained to use software products that had been developed by us for decades and may have got outdated by now. I believe that I have managed to surprise our international colleagues with interesting Russian developments in the sphere of bipedal walking robots and got a boost of motivation and new ideas in return. In addition, during the showcase sessions I had a number of meetings with colleagues where we discussed possibilities to expand our research cooperation, in particular, with [Kyoto University](http://www.kyoto-u.ac.jp/en) and [University of the West of England](http://www.uwe.ac.uk/). Furthermore, I had important negotiations with representatives of [DJI](http://www.dji.com/) and [PAL Robotics](http://pal-robotics.com/ru/), the content of which I cannot reveal yet” – shared his impressions Prof. Magid.

“I hope you had a good chance to advertise your new and rapidly developed University and lab to other robotics researchers. We look forward to seeing some nice work from your team since it seems that the development is impressively fast, and also to visit you in the future” - said one of the workshop organizers, Dr. Dimitrios Kanoulas, Istituto Italiano di Tecnologia (IIT), Genoa.
