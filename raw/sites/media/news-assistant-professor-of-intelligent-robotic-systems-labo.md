## Assistant Professor of Intelligent Robotic Systems Laboratory Ilya Afanasyev took part in Extreme Robotics conference
9 октября 2015
<p class="MsoNormal">The event took place in Saint Petersburg (Russia), October 8-9, 2015.</p>
![](https://media.innopolis.university/upload/iblock/314/1.jpg)
[Extreme Robotics conference](http://er.rtc.ru/index.php/en/) (ER-2015) is one of the most important scientific events in the field of robotics in Russia. [Russian Association for Artificial Intelligence](http://raai.org/) has organized the second [Unmanned Vehicles with Artificial Intelligence Elements](http://www.ai-uv.ru/) in the framework of ER-2015. The workshop brought together researchers, engineers and experts interested in development and intellectualization of unmanned vehicles.
Assistant Professor of Innopolis University Ilya Afanasyev presented the scientific paper “[Maze navigation of a heterogeneous group of robots (UAVs and UGVs) with probabilistic roadmap in Gazebo 3D simulator](http://www.ai-uv.ru/program)”, prepared by Intelligent Robotic Systems Laboratory members (Ilya Afanasyev, Artur Sagitov, Igor Danilov and Evgeni Magid). The paper is indexed in [Russian Science Citation Index database](http://elibrary.ru/) (bibliographic database of scientific publications in Russian).
“Currently there are very few professional conferences in the field of robotics in Russia. Therefore, “Extreme Robotics” Conference was the platform, which brought together experts from across the European part of the country for discussing various robotics issues, even those which are not strictly related to “extreme systems”. The conference held closed-door and public sessions. Closed-door session brought together researchers who works on the projects for [Ministry of Defence](http://eng.mil.ru/), [Ministry of Emergency Situations](http://en.mchs.ru/) and [Russian Federal Space Agency](http://en.federalspace.ru/). On the open session “Unmanned Vehicles with Artificial Intelligence Elements” less than one-third of scientific reports were directly dedicated to the Artificial Intelligence, which explains the difficulty of implementation of these systems in robotics. Another interesting fact was that most of the reports presented quadcopters as unmanned vehicles, which highlights the trend of using the cheapest robotic platforms. I was pleased to note that at least one third of the participants used ROS (Robot Operating System) to control robots or robotic simulators, which shows the movement of Russian researchers in line with the global trend of using standardized software packages for robot control”, shared his impressions Assistant Professor Ilya Afanasyev.
